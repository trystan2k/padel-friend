## 1. Owner-approved decisions (binding)

Recorded as approved by the owner. Later units implement them; changing any requires a new owner decision.

1. **Deletion strategy.** Hard-delete all active account data and the Auth user. Historic shared records are irreversibly anonymized through an internal random tombstone: a new UUID unrelated to the old Auth UUID, with no old-to-new mapping persisted. Other players' official scores, ratings and replay inputs are preserved.
2. **Ownership fallback.** If no consenting successor exists, an orphaned community is archived ownerless; unsupported future activity is retired/cancelled; a league or match with protected unresolved results is closed/frozen, preserving official results.
3. **Disclosure threshold.** Retained history shows a generic localized placeholder ("Former player"-class). No stable cross-match identifier, no profile/directory/search presence, no photo.
4. **Case retention.** Operator case records are purged down to non-identifying outcome counts **90 days after the final response**.
5. **Third parties.** Supabase erasure is automated. Cloudflare, Resend, Google and browser Push providers are documented manual runbook steps (runbook is unit E).
6. **Privacy policy.** Truthful update now; legal review flagged pending (external, owner-owned); no compliance assertions.

### 2.2 Topology evidence (how derived and what was found)

Method (LC): read-only queries against the local TEST database catalog:

- `pg_constraint` where `contype = 'f'` and `confrelid` in (`auth.users`, `public.player_profiles`), reporting table, columns and `confdeltype`;

- `pg_attribute` for every public relation with uuid, uuid[], jsonb, json or identifying-text columns;
- `pg_policies` for `storage.objects`; `storage.buckets`; `cron.job`.

Legend.
**Subject role**: AH = account holder (claimed Auth + profile); PI = provisional identity (Auth user, not claimed); UI = unclaimed invitee / awaiting-identity (email only, no Auth user); AR = actor reference (subject appears as actor/target on someone else's record).
**Controller/access/processor**: the operator (Thiago Mendonça, Spain) is controller of application data; Supabase is processor hosting DB/Auth/Storage; browsers access through RLS/RPC; server code and the operator use the service role.
**Erasure**: HD hard delete; RK re-key to internal tombstone anchor (unit B), retaining other players' fact; SC scrub payload keys / free text; PG purge; REV operator content review.
**Proof**: assertion scans over classified FK and non-FK locations, byte-level storage checks, Auth absence, replay parity.
Retention column: "none evidenced" means no expiry/purge in migration source (MS), not that none exists in production.

## 7. Privacy request process definition

Operational guidance. The operator is Thiago Mendonça (Spain). The published contact address is the `PRIVACY_CONTACT_EMAIL` constant in `src/features/privacy/privacy-content.ts`; this document refers to it as "the privacy mailbox" and must stay in step with that constant.

### 7.1 Intake routes

- **In-app (signed-in, non-anonymous, non-provisional account holders):** own-profile privacy entry (unit D); self-only; the database derives the subject from the verified session.
- **Email to the privacy mailbox:** unauthenticated users, accounts that cannot sign in, unclaimed invitees, representatives, and every non-erasure right (access, rectification, restriction, objection, portability). A claim or sign-up is never required to exercise a right.
- Record received time and scope; confirm whether a message is the same right or a related separate request. Operators do not treat all rights as erasure.

### 7.2 Acknowledgement and neutral handling

Acknowledge promptly with a **neutral case reference**. Do not confirm or deny that an account, invitation or provisional record exists before verification. Cover absences so the mailbox and queue are always owned.

### 7.3 Identity verification

1. For an account: the operator reads the address **on file in Auth** (never the address in the request payload) and sends a single-use, short-lived random challenge to that address from the operator mailbox. Execution requires the valid challenge plus explicit confirmation; for in-app cases the proof binds to the same case and account.
2. Store only a digest, expiry and proof result; never the raw code in the DB or logs. Expired or consumed proof is cancelled; attempts are limited; a change of email, identity or target state forces new verification.
3. For provisional/unclaimed/awaiting-identity people: challenge the address independently of any Auth claim; check server-side that the invitation/addition really links to that address; match private records by verified email only (never public search); scope all matches and resolve duplicates/aliases deliberately.
4. If the mailbox is lost, the address is mistyped or recycled, the request is ambiguous, or an agent submits it: do not execute and do not disclose existence. Use **proportionate alternative evidence** or representation checks (for example, control of a signed-in session, or facts only the account holder would know); Art. 12(6) GDPR permits requesting additional information only where reasonable doubts exist. Escalate unresolved cases. **Never solicit passwords or routine ID copies.**
5. Authorization never comes from request text, an old session alone, `email_confirmed_at` alone, knowledge of an invitation, or editable JWT metadata.

### 7.4 Timing, exceptions, response

Operational guidance based on GDPR Art. 12 (not a compliance claim): act on and respond to a request **without undue delay and in any event within one month** of receipt; the period may be **extended by up to two further months** where necessary for complexity or number of requests, with the requester informed of the extension and the reasons **within the first month**. If no action is taken, inform the requester of the reasons and of the complaint and judicial-remedy options (competent authority for Spain: AEPD). Do not restart the clock merely because verification was needed.

- **Erasure (Art. 17):** assess grounds and exceptions per case; record the refusal or partial-retention ground (for example, retained historic records of **other** players under decision 1 and section 8; legal claims).
- **Recipient notification (Art. 19):** assess whether recipients (section 5) received the data and whether notifying is possible without disproportionate effort.
- **Other rights (Arts. 15-16, 18, 20-21):** manual, with redaction of other persons' data and secure delivery of any export.
- **Final response:** over the verified email thread, not via login. State what was removed, what remains and why, outstanding processor/backup limits, and how to challenge. Confirm only proven phases; incomplete external work stays tracked.

### 7.5 Security of the process

Mailbox protected with MFA; no forwarding to shared or public spaces; no personal data in tickets or logs beyond the case record; minimal evidence retention, destroyed when no longer needed; case records follow decision 4 (non-identifying outcome counts 90 days after the final response). A random case id remains personal data while correlated with retained mail; do not call it anonymous.

### 7.6 Escalation

Escalate to the owner and, where appropriate, qualified legal advice: legal uncertainty or legal holds; suspected security incident or data breach; urgent safety-related cases (harassment/stalking context); contested identity; refusal grounds; minors or representation questions. No legal certification is claimed.

## 8. Pseudonymization versus anonymization

- A replaced UUID is **pseudonymous** while any mapping or reasonably available linkage remains. Anonymization requires no reasonably likely re-identification **in context**.
- Linkage sources to test: old API/log exports; a known roster, date or venue in a small community or league; a unique rating trail; the subject named in another user's free text; previous standings snapshots; position in a four-player match.
- **Assessment method:** with an authorized member, opponent and community admin who know the retired UUID and contextual facts, plus a guest or other-community view, try to identify the subject from retained data and readable surfaces. Pass only if none can by reasonable means. Remove local joins and maps, redundant context, free text and stable cross-match display keys; use scope-local display keys unrelated to the internal UUID.
- **If the record fails as retained:** restrict exposure first, then suppress or aggregate the failing fields or rows where safe for official results; if neither is possible, document the exact trade-off and **block the case pending an owner decision**. Do not relabel pseudonymized data as anonymous.
- **Conflict rule:** where fidelity for other players (replay inputs, standings, ratings) and anonymization cannot both be achieved, surface the specific evidence and obtain an explicit scope decision. Participants cannot be made to forget what they observed; do not promise that.
